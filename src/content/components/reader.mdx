## How it works

- `columns-1` plus a fixed `h-80` makes the prose fragment: content that doesn't fit the
  height goes into another column beside it, and `overflow-x-auto` makes those columns
  scrollable. One column box is one page. `[column-fill:auto]` fills each page top to
  bottom instead of balancing the last one short.
- **`::column` is the page.** It's the one handle CSS has ever had on a generated column
  box, and it accepts scroll-snap properties only — `scroll-snap-align: center` and
  `scroll-snap-stop: always` turn scrolling into page turns; nothing that affects layout
  applies to it.
- `scroll-marker-group: after` plus a marker on `::column` gives every page a dot the
  browser generates, orders, and keeps in sync; `:target-current` marks the page you're
  on. Clicking a dot scrolls to that page.
- Keep `column-gap` at least as large as the container's side padding — `px-8` with
  `gap-x-12` here — or the next page peeks past the edge, since centered snapping splits
  the container's inline padding evenly into the two gutters.
- `hyphens-auto` needs the `lang` attribute to pick a dictionary; `break-after-avoid` on
  the heading keeps a title off the bottom of a page.
- Prev/next buttons are the same `::scroll-button()` recipe as [Carousel](/components/carousel);
  the dots already reach every page, so this demo leaves them out.

## Why the CSS block

`::column` and `::scroll-marker` are generated — there's no element to put a class on —
and the marker needs `content`, which has no utility syntax. [Carousel](/components/carousel)
makes the same exception for its dots and arrows. The block is optional: skip it and you
keep a scrollable block of columns, just without snap points or page dots.

## Accessibility

- Pagination is layout only. The text stays in one continuous DOM order, so a screen
  reader reads the whole piece and nothing is hidden or duplicated.
- `tabindex="0"` plus the focus ring makes the article keyboard-focusable. Some engines
  focus scrollable regions on their own; the attribute makes it explicit everywhere.
  Arrow keys turn pages, and mandatory snapping lands each press on a page boundary.
- The generated dots form a focusgroup: the whole row is one tab stop, and arrow keys
  move between dots and scroll the reader.
- The heading names the region through `aria-labelledby="rdr-title"`.
- `motion-safe:scroll-smooth`, not plain `scroll-smooth` — the global reduced-motion rule
  only clamps transition and animation duration, and a dot click would otherwise slide a
  full page past someone who asked for less motion.
- Size the box in `rem`, not a viewport unit, so a larger font size produces more pages
  instead of clipped text.

## The fallback story

Without `::column` the generated columns can't be targeted, so there are no snap points
and no dots. The article is still a horizontally scrollable block of columns with an
ordinary scrollbar — that's why the scrollbar is hidden only behind
`supports-[selector(::column)]:[scrollbar-width:none]`, so engines without dots keep
their one way to tell there's more to scroll. Without `scroll-marker-group` you lose the
dots and keep the scrolling and snapping.
